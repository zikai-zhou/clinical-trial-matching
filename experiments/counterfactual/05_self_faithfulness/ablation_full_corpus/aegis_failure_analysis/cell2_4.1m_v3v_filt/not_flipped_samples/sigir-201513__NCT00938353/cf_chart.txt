A 6-month-old boy presents to the emergency department with complaints of progressively worsening dysphagia, drooling, fever, and vocal changes. He is toxic-appearing and leans forward while sitting on his mother's lap. He is drooling and speaks with a muffled "hot potato" voice. The parents deny the possibility of foreign body ingestion or trauma, and they report that they are delaying some of his vaccines. On arrival, he is noted to have a barking cough. He is diagnosed with croup. The patient was brought to the emergency department due to the acute onset of barking cough and respiratory distress.
