A 72-year-old man complains of calf pain when walking uphill, which has remained unchanged over the past 3 months. The patient had an uncomplicated myocardial infarction 2 years earlier and a transient ischemic attack more than 6 months ago. Over the past month, his blood pressure has been stable with diltiazem, hydrochlorothiazide, and aspirin. His current medications are isosorbide dinitrate, hydrochlorothiazide, and aspirin. On physical examination, his blood pressure is 151/91 mm Hg, and his heart rate is not recorded. There is a right carotid bruit. His lower extremities are slightly cool to the touch and have diminished pulses at the dorsalis pedis.
