A 62-year-old man sees a neurologist for progressive memory loss and jerking movements of the lower extremities. He carries a diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease, established by United Kingdom Brain Bank criteria. He reports recurrent episodes of freezing of gait in the past and currently, though none were witnessed during today’s visit. Neurologic examination confirms severe cognitive deficits and memory dysfunction. An electroencephalogram shows generalized periodic sharp waves. Neuroimaging studies show moderately advanced cerebral atrophy. A cortical biopsy shows diffuse vacuolar changes of the gray matter with reactive astrocytosis but no inflammatory infiltration. Despite these cognitive deficits, he is judged able to understand instructions and complete study procedures with support, so the dementia does not preclude participation in the required study protocol.
