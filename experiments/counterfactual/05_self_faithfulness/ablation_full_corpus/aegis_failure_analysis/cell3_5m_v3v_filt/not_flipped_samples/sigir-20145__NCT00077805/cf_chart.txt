A 56-year-old female on 20th day post-left mastectomy presents to the emergency department complaining of shortness of breath and malaise. Over the last 24 hours, she developed sudden-onset right lower-limb weakness and slurred speech. The patient says that she has remained in bed for the last two weeks. The physical examination reveals tenderness on the left upper thoracic wall and right calf. Neurologic examination shows marked right lower-limb paresis with inability to lift the leg against gravity and inability to bear weight; she requires assistance for transfers. The surgical incision shows no bleeding or signs of infection. No cerebrovascular accident is documented at this time, and no acute cerebrovascular accident is noted. Pulmonary auscultation is significant for bilateral decreased breath sounds, especially at the right base. Laboratory tests reveal an elevated D-dimer. These new focal neurologic findings are consistent with an acute ischemic cerebral infarction. Her recent confirmed malignancy and postoperative recovery are noted; these are not expected to compromise follow-up or outcome assessment.
