A 40-year-old woman with no past medical history presents to the ER with excruciating pain in her right arm that had started 1 hour prior to her admission. She denies trauma. On examination she is in moderate discomfort but not pale, breathing comfortably; extremities are warm with brisk capillary refill. Her body temperature is normal and her blood pressure is 80/60. There is no clinical evidence of cardiogenic shock. Her right arm has no discoloration or movement limitation.
