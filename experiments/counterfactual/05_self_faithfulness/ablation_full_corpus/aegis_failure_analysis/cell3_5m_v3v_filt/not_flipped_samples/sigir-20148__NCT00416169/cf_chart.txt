A 62-year-old man sees a neurologist for progressive memory loss and language decline. Neurologic examination confirms severe cognitive deficits and memory dysfunction. He carries a diagnosis of Pick complex that has been documented for more than one year, and he is currently diagnosed with primary progressive aphasia. Magnetic resonance imaging demonstrates frontotemporal-predominant lobar atrophy consistent with Pick complex. No alternative degenerative cause of his dementia has been established.
