A 62-year-old man sees a neurologist for progressive memory loss, jerking movements of the lower extremities, and episodes of freezing of gait. Neurologic examination confirms severe cognitive deficits and memory dysfunction. The patient is noted to have a history of freezing of gait, which is also present currently. He meets the United Kingdom Brain Bank criteria for a diagnosis of Parkinson's disease. An electroencephalogram shows generalized periodic sharp waves. Neuroimaging studies show moderately advanced cerebral atrophy. A cortical biopsy shows diffuse vacuolar changes of the gray matter with reactive astrocytosis but no inflammatory infiltration.
